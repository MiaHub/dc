"Lack of sleep ✅" was on the list of bad habits in [DailyRoutine]({{ site.baseurl }}/2023/01/02/dailyroutine.html)
without any note on how to actually fix it. This is that note.

### Why it sits above the rest

Sleep is the one habit that makes every other habit easier. Diet, training, focus and
patience all degrade first when sleep is short — and the degradation is invisible from
the inside, which is the dangerous part. You do not feel impaired. You feel fine and
perform worse.

That alone is the argument for treating it as a fixed constraint rather than the thing
that gets cut when the day runs long.

### What actually moves the needle

**A constant wake time.** The single highest-value change, and the least popular one.
Getting up at the same time every day — including weekends — does more than any evening
routine, because it is the wake time that anchors the rhythm, not the bedtime.

**Light in the morning.** Daylight shortly after waking sets the clock. In a Bavarian
winter that means going outside anyway, on the grey days especially.

**Caffeine has a long tail.** It stays in the system for hours. A coffee in the afternoon
is still working at bedtime, whether or not it is keeping you awake — it costs depth of
sleep even when you fall asleep fine. My cut-off is early afternoon.

**A cool, dark, quiet room.** Cheap to do, easy to check, easy to keep ignoring.

**Alcohol is not a sleep aid.** It shortens the time to fall asleep and wrecks the second
half of the night. Already crossed off the list for other reasons.

### The screen problem

The hard one for anyone in this job. Not only the light — the *engagement*. A problem you
were still turning over at 23:00 does not stop being interesting because you closed the
laptop.

What works for me is a hard stop and writing the open thread down. Getting it out of my head
and into the vault is what actually lets me put it down; trusting that it will be there
tomorrow is the whole point of keeping notes in the first place.

### The honest part

I know all of this and still get it wrong during a migration week. The goal is not
perfection, it is noticing quickly and returning to the constant wake time — that one
anchor pulls the rest back on its own.
