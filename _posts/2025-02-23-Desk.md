Notes on the workstation. This is the health topic with the most direct overlap with the
job — eight hours a day in one chair, for years, is a bigger input than anything I do at
the weekend.

### The setup

**Monitor top edge at or just below eye level.** If you look down at a screen all day, your
neck holds your head forward all day. A laptop on a desk is always wrong for this; it needs
a stand and a separate keyboard.

**Elbows roughly at a right angle**, forearms supported, shoulders down. If your shoulders
are up near your ears, the desk is too high or the chair too low.

**Feet flat on the floor.** If they do not reach, the chair is too high and a footrest is
the fix, not sitting on one leg.

**Screen at about arm's length.** Closer than that and your eyes hold a strong focus all
day; further and you lean in, which undoes the neck position above.

### Eyes

The rule I actually remember: **20-20-20** — every 20 minutes, look at something about
20 feet away for 20 seconds. The point is releasing the focus muscles, which do not get a
break otherwise.

Two things that help more than they should: turn the screen brightness down to match the
room instead of fighting it, and blink deliberately. People blink markedly less while
concentrating, which is most of why screen work makes eyes feel dry.

### The real fix is not the chair

No posture survives four hours. The best chair in the world is still one position, and one
position is the problem.

What works is changing position often — standing for calls, walking for anything that does
not need a screen, getting up properly at least once an hour. A cheap desk you leave
regularly beats an expensive one you never get out of.

I use compile and deploy waits as the trigger. There is a natural pause in the work already;
using it to stand up costs nothing.

### Wrists

Keyboard flat rather than propped up on its feet, wrists straight rather than bent back,
mouse close enough that the elbow stays at your side instead of reaching forward. Reaching
for a mouse all day is a shoulder problem before it is a wrist problem.

### What I would tell a new developer

None of this hurts at 25. All of it is a bill that arrives later, and by then the habits are
twenty years old. Setting the monitor height correctly takes ten minutes once.
