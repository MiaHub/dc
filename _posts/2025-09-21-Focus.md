[DailyRoutine]({{ site.baseurl }}/2023/01/02/dailyroutine.html) covers habits — the things
you do without deciding. This is the other half: what to do with the hours where deciding
is the entire job.

### Attention is the scarce resource

Not time. I have the same hours as everyone; what runs out is the capacity for the kind of
thinking that solves a hard problem. That capacity is largest early, degrades through the
day, and cannot be topped up by wanting it more.

Which reframes the planning question. Not "what do I have time for" but "what deserves the
good hours". Debugging a production issue deserves them. Answering mail does not.

### Context switching is the tax

The cost of an interruption is not its duration. It is the reconstruction afterwards —
rebuilding the model of the problem you were holding. A two-minute question can cost twenty
minutes of work.

This is, incidentally, the same argument I make about tooling on the technical side. Removing
the switch from editor to 5250 session was never mainly about keystrokes; it was about not
dropping the thread.

Being Head of IT means being interruptible by design, so the answer cannot be "no
interruptions". It is to defend *some* blocks rather than pretend to defend all of them, and
to be genuinely available in between rather than half-present throughout.

### Write down where you were

The habit that pays for itself: before stepping away, note what you were doing and what the
next step was. One line is enough.

Reconstruction is the expensive part, and a sentence written while the context is still in
your head removes almost all of it. It is the same reason the vault exists at all — you do
not have to know everything, you have to know where to find it.

### Things that quietly destroy it

- **Notifications.** Anything that can pull attention will, and the answer is off rather
  than disciplined
- **Too many open threads.** Five half-finished things cost more attention than five queued
  ones, because the unfinished ones keep asking
- **Short sleep.** [It comes back to this]({{ site.baseurl }}/2025/01/12/Sleep.html). Tired
  attention feels like normal attention from the inside
- **Working longer to catch up.** The hours added at the end are the least capable ones, and
  they are borrowed from tomorrow's good hours

### Stopping is a skill

The hardest thing to learn was that ending the day deliberately is part of the work. Not
stopping when finished — you are never finished — but stopping at a planned point, writing
the thread down, and letting the problem run in the background.

Solutions arrive on the drive home surprisingly often. They do not arrive during the
eleventh hour at the desk.
