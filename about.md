<h2 class="pf-section-title">How I work</h2>

<p>
My background is physics and technical drafting rather than a computer-science degree, and I
think that shows in how I approach software: I want to understand the actual process before I
model it, and I would rather ship something stable and maintainable than something clever.
Most of my work is translating business requirements into system solutions that still make
sense to the next person who opens them.
</p>

<p>
The other half of the job is modernisation. I am the one at KAPS who replaced the traditional
IBM i toolchain with VS Code and Git, who introduced branching and traceable development states
to a codebase that had never had them, and who has been responsible for AI integration across
the company since 2023 &mdash; including in-house MCP tools that let an assistant issue compile
commands on the IBM i directly. Legacy platforms do not have to stay legacy in how you work on them.
</p>

<p>
This site is the other side of that instinct. It is an Obsidian vault published through Jekyll,
and it exists because of two lines I keep coming back to:
</p>

<blockquote>
  <p>You do not have to know everything. You just have to know where to find it.<br><em>&mdash; Albert Einstein</em></p>
  <p>A problem well stated is half solved.<br><em>&mdash; Charles Kettering</em></p>
</blockquote>
